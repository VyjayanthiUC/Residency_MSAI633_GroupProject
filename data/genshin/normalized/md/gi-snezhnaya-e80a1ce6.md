Snezhnaya ( Russian : Снежная Snezhnaya , "Snowy" ) is one of the seven nations of Teyvat and one of the major regions in Genshin Impact . It is the nation that worships the Tsaritsa , the Cryo Archon and God of Love. The Fatui , a military force under her control, is particularly notorious for their unwavering devotion and unscrupulous methods in enacting her will. The autonomous region Nod-Krai is located at the southernmost part of Snezhnaya.

Archon Quest Chapter VII takes place in this region.


## How to Access

Access to Snezhnaya is unlocked after completing the Archon Quest Prologue : Act III - Song of the Dragon and Freedom , but only a small part of Volkodlak Tundra is available. A Teleport Waypoint in Volkodlak Tundra is automatically unlocked and is where Chapter VII is automatically accepted. A very small area surrounding Sretomorozsk is available once players start Archon Quest Gunfire in the Silent Lands in Chapter VII : Act I - Everwinter Without Mercy , and the entire nation becomes accessible once said quest is completed. Players will be quickly overwhelmed by the sheer cold if they attempt to progress without meeting the requirements, as the borders of the restricted area behave like a Subzero Zone .


## Description

Snezhnaya is a region of perpetual winter storms; Nadia states that blizzards can last for a month straight and despite being in the midst of danger, its people look up to the sky and are just happy to be alive. Tartaglia claims that people would literally freeze to death if they didn't keep moving outside. At night, the Snezhnayan sky can see a beautiful aurora of dancing light. Each settlement within Snezhnaya contains a Kresnik's Torch , which are fueled by Kresnik Kristall .

Technologically, Snezhnaya is much more advanced than the other regions on Teyvat. Venti states that the Fatui are the strongest out of the seven regions' military, which is supported by the fact that all of their known fighting members utilize elemental attacks. The description for Agent's Sacrificial Knife calls it a "weapon made with superior Snezhnayan technology." The Fatui Skirmishers wear mechanical suits of varying degrees, and some of its members use guns and cannons in battle. Alice , who has visited Snezhnaya, recalls seeing massive factories, which she wrote down in her Teyvat Travel Guide , and Il Dottore built a factory in Liyue to conduct Ruin Guard research which is fully automated.

Snezhnaya is stated to be considered the same status as Mondstadt , as an independent and not belonging to any other nation, according to Jean .

The Adventurers' Guild originates from Snezhnaya; while this fact doesn't seem to be a secret, few people comment on it and the Guild doesn't face the same apprehension that people regard the Fatui with. It was also hinted that Katheryne , the receptionist(s) for the Adventurers' Guild, are a series of androids created for the role: the Katherynes in all regions so far are identical, and have "Error..." and "Rebooting..." idle quotes. This is further emphasized by the Katheryne stationed in Liyue, as she side-steps the Traveler questioning about her identity. Katheryne's identity as a "bionic Snezhnayan puppet" was confirmed by Nahida .

## Trivia › Etymology

- "Snezhnaya" ( Russian : Снежная Snezhnaya , "Snowy" ) is a Russian adjective for "snowy," expecting a feminine noun to follow (most likely Страна Strana or Земля Zemlya — Russian words for "country" or "land" respectively, both of which are feminine and are permitted to be omitted for a synecdoche in such a context). The game's voiceover places the accent on the second syllable (Snezh- na -ya); however, in the original word, it's on the first ( Snezh -na-ya). During the first Closed Beta Test, Snezhnaya was instead named "Prekrasnaya" ( Прекрасная Prekrasnaya , "Beautiful" ). Snezhnaya's Chinese name, 至冬 Zhìdōng , is an anagram of the Chinese term for winter solstice ( 冬至 Dōngzhì ).
- During the first Closed Beta Test, Snezhnaya was instead named "Prekrasnaya" ( Прекрасная Prekrasnaya , "Beautiful" ).
- Snezhnaya's Chinese name, 至冬 Zhìdōng , is an anagram of the Chinese term for winter solstice ( 冬至 Dōngzhì ).
- "Zapolyarny" ( Russian : Заполярный Zapolyarnyy , "Beyond the Arctic Circle" ) roughly translates to "beyond the Arctic Circle" or "inside the Polar region."
- "Morepesok" ( Russian : Морепесок Morepesok , "Sea-Sand" ) is a compound of the words for море more , "sea" and песок pesok , "sand," itself referring to a beach or coastline as a sort of kenning.
- "Snezhnograd" ( Russian : Снежноград Snezhnograd , "Snowy City" ) uses the Russian root - град -grad , "city," which was traditionally used when establishing cities in Russia.
- The name "Novokitezhgrad" ( Russian : Новокитежград Novokitezhgrad , "New Kitezh City" ) contains the name Kitezh , a legendary city in central Russia that sank into a lake, similar to Atlantis in Greek mythology.
- "Tsaritsa" ( Russian : Царица Tsaritsa , "Tsarina" ), used to refer to their Archon, is the feminine form of Tsar , which ultimately derives from Caesar, a title of Roman emperors. So far, no one has called her by her true name; Snezhnayans call her by her title, while Venti and Zhongli call her "the Cryo Archon."


## Trivia › Cultural References

- Snezhnaya is primarily inspired by by a mix of Medieval, Tsarist and Soviet Russia (primarily Russian Arctic and Siberia in regards to landscape), and often uses Russian words for names. However, it also draws from other European nations as well, such as using Italian commedia dell'arte stock characters' names for the Harbingers.
- Snezhnaya's affiliation with brutal winters parallels Russia's climate, which has famously contributed to the failures of the many military campaigns made against it.
- Tartaglia addresses the Traveler as "comrade" in his voice-overs , the usual translation of "tovarishch" ( Russian : товарищ tovarishch ) used in Soviet/Bolshevik Russia. In the Japanese version of Childe's Character Miscellany, he starts off his letter ("Dearest sister...") in Russian.
- In the Japanese version of Childe's Character Miscellany, he starts off his letter ("Dearest sister...") in Russian.
- Fire-Water ( Russian : огненная вода ognennaya voda , "fiery water" ), which Angel's Share bartender Charles deems the strongest wine he knows of, originates from Snezhnaya. It is a reference to the liquor vodka , a drink commonly associated with Russia. In real life, Russian Vodka is normally 45-55% Ethanol, and some Vodkas are more than 80% Ethanol, making Vodka one of the most alcoholic drinks in the world.
